4. What a drawn signature is, and what it isn't

This is the section that matters. A signature image generated here is a picture of your signature. It is convenient, it is normal for everyday paperwork, and it is what most people mean when they say "sign this PDF".

It is not proof of identity, and it is not a certified or qualified electronic signature. It carries no cryptographic binding to you, no timestamp from a trusted authority, and no audit trail. Anyone holding the file could put that image on another document.

Many legal systems accept a simple electronic signature for ordinary contracts and internal paperwork — the EU's eIDAS regulation and the US ESIGN Act both recognise the category. But certain documents specifically require a qualified or certified signature, a notarised signature, or a witnessed one: property transfers, some employment and immigration paperwork, court filings, and anything a counterparty insists on.

Check what the document actually requires before you sign it. If it asks for more than a signature image, it needs a dedicated e-signature service that verifies identity — not this site. Nothing on this page is legal advice.
